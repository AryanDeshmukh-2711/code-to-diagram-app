The Library Management System is a web application used by a university library
to manage its physical collection and the people who borrow from it. The library
holds books, each identified by an ISBN and recorded with a title, an author, a
publication year and the number of copies currently available. Every book
belongs to exactly one category, such as Fiction, Reference or Periodicals, and
a category groups many books.

Members are registered library users. A member record holds a membership number,
a name, an email address, a join date and whether the membership is currently
active. Librarians are staff members who operate the lending desk; each
librarian has a staff identifier and an assigned desk. Both members and
librarians are people, and share a name and an email address. An administrator
manages member accounts and system configuration.

A member borrows a book by presenting it at the lending desk, where a librarian
scans the ISBN. The system checks that the member is active, has not reached the
borrowing limit and has no outstanding fines above the threshold, then creates a
loan with a due date and reduces the number of available copies. Returning a
book closes the loan and restores the copy count. If a book is returned after
its due date, the system raises a fine against the member, recording the amount,
the reason and the date it was paid.

When no copy of a book is available, a member may place a reservation, which
records the date it was placed and the date it expires. A reservation is either
pending, ready for collection, collected, expired or cancelled. Librarians also
maintain the catalogue by adding, editing and withdrawing books, and manage
member accounts.

The system must be available during library opening hours and must record every
loan and return so that the collection can be audited.
